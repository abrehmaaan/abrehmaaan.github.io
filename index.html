About

Abdul Rehman Akbar is a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at The Ohio State University and a Graduate Research Associate in the AI4Path Lab (Department of Pathology, OSU Wexner Medical Center), advised by Dr. Muhammad Khalid Khan Niazi. His research is in computational pathology: developing interpretable deep learning models that analyze H&E-stained whole-slide images for pan-cancer prognosis, treatment-response prediction, molecular subtyping, and genomic mutation inference. He has worked on lung, colorectal, pancreatic, and breast cancer.

His work centers on a simple question: how much molecular and outcome information can be recovered from the routine pathology slide every cancer patient already has? He works on multiple-instance learning, multiscale spatial modeling of the tumor microenvironment, pathology foundation models, and large language model systems for pathology reports, with a focus on tools that can be deployed in clinical workflows.

Before his PhD, he was Product Owner for AI at Expertflow, where he led development of large language model solutions for contact centers across text and voice channels. He holds a BS in Computer Science from Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad.

Research Programs

PathRosetta
Lung cancer recurrence
A spatially aware, multiscale cell-ecology framework that predicts 5-year recurrence in invasive lung adenocarcinoma from H&E whole-slide images by modeling how cells organize into neighborhoods and tissue. Published in npj Digital Medicine (2026).
GenoGlyph
Pan-cancer mutation inference
Infers clinically actionable gene mutations and risk groups directly from routine diagnostic H&E slides across solid tumor types, as a tissue-sparing triage step before sequencing.
PanSubNet
Pancreatic cancer subtyping
Predicts basal-like versus classical molecular subtypes of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma from H&E slides, without RNA sequencing. Published in Communications Medicine (2026).
PathoScribe
LLM pathology intelligence
A retrieval-augmented large language model framework that turns archives of surgical pathology reports into a searchable library for case retrieval, cohort building, and report generation.
